1.8 Introductory Organic Chemistry

Hazards, functional groups, nomenclature, reactions and structural isomers

1.8 Introductory Organic Chemistry

本页对应 1.8 Introductory Organic Chemistry.pdf.pdf，整理 hazards & risks、functional groups、homologous series、IUPAC nomenclature、reaction classification 和 structural isomerism。专业词汇保留 English，示例尽量使用 structural / displayed formula 说明。

Learning objectives

学完本页后，你应该能够：

  • interpret common hazard symbols and describe safe laboratory practice；
  • identify important functional groups and homologous series；
  • name organic compounds using IUPAC rules；
  • classify organic reactions as addition、substitution、elimination、oxidation or reduction；
  • draw and identify chain、positional and functional-group structural isomers。

1.8.1 Hazards and risks

Hazard and risk

A hazard is something that can cause harm。A risk is the likelihood that the hazard will cause harm under specific conditions。

Risk depends on factors such as：

  • the toxicity or corrosiveness of a substance；
  • the amount and concentration used；
  • the route of exposure：skin、eyes、inhalation or ingestion；
  • the temperature、pressure and volatility；
  • the control measures used in the laboratory。

Common hazard symbols

Symbol Meaning Typical precaution
Exclamation mark Harmful / irritant Avoid skin and eye contact；use gloves and eye protection
Corrosive Causes skin burns or serious eye damage Wear goggles and gloves；handle carefully
Flame Flammable Keep away from ignition sources；use a fume cupboard where needed
Skull and crossbones Acute toxicity Avoid all exposure；use the smallest practicable quantity

Risk assessment

A good risk assessment should：

  1. identify the hazards of each chemical and procedure；
  2. identify who or what could be harmed；
  3. evaluate the likelihood and severity of harm；
  4. choose control measures；
  5. record the remaining risk and review the procedure。

Useful control measures include using a fume cupboard、wearing eye protection、using a smaller volume、keeping flammable substances away from flames、washing spills immediately and disposing of waste correctly。

1.8.2 Functional groups and homologous series

Functional groups

A functional group is an atom or group of atoms that determines the characteristic chemical reactions of an organic compound。

Family Functional group General representation
Alkene Carbon-carbon double bond C=C
Halogenoalkane Halogen atom R-X，X = F, Cl, Br, I
Alcohol Hydroxyl group R-OH
Aldehyde Aldehyde group R-CHO
Ketone Carbonyl group within chain R-CO-R
Carboxylic acid Carboxyl group R-COOH
Ester Ester group R-COO-R
Primary amine Amino group R-NH₂
Nitrile Cyano group R-CN

The functional group is often the most reactive part of the molecule and determines the characteristic reactions of the homologous series。

Homologous series

A homologous series is a family of compounds with：

  • the same functional group；
  • the same general formula；
  • similar chemical properties；
  • successive members differing by CH₂；
  • gradual changes in physical properties such as boiling point。

For alkanes：

\[ \text{general formula}=\ce{C_nH_{2n+2}} \]

The boiling points of alkanes generally increase as chain length increases because larger molecules have more electrons and stronger London dispersion forces。

1.8.3 Nomenclature and classification

Parent-chain names

Choose the longest continuous carbon chain containing the principal functional group or multiple bond。The root names are：

Number of carbons Root Alkane
1 meth- methane
2 eth- ethane
3 prop- propane
4 but- butane
5 pent- pentane
6 hex- hexane
7 hept- heptane
8 oct- octane
9 non- nonane
10 dec- decane

IUPAC naming steps

  1. Find the longest carbon chain。
  2. Number the chain from the end that gives the principal functional group the lowest possible number。
  3. Identify substituents and their positions。
  4. Identify the suffix for the principal functional group or multiple bond。
  5. Use prefixes such as di-、tri- and tetra- for repeated substituents。
  6. Arrange different substituents in alphabetical order；ignore di-、tri- and tetra- when alphabetising。
  7. Use hyphens between numbers and words，and commas between numbers。

For an alkene，the chain must contain the C=C bond and the double bond receives the lowest possible number：

The correct name is 3-methylhex-2-ene，not 4-methylhex-2-ene。

Substituents

Common alkyl substituents include：

\[ \ce{-CH3}=\text{methyl},\qquad\ce{-CH2CH3}=\text{ethyl} \]

Functional-group suffixes

Family Suffix Example
Alkane -ane propane
Alkene -ene propene
Alcohol -ol ethanol
Aldehyde -al propanal
Ketone -one propanone
Carboxylic acid -oic acid propanoic acid
Ester -yl alkanoate ethyl ethanoate
Amine -amine ethylamine
Nitrile -nitrile ethanenitrile

Primary、secondary and tertiary compounds

For an alcohol，classification depends on the number of carbon groups attached to the carbon bearing the -OH group：

  • primary (1°)：attached to one other carbon；
  • secondary (2°)：attached to two other carbons；
  • tertiary (3°)：attached to three other carbons。

The position of the functional group should be included in the name，for example butan-1-ol and butan-2-ol。

1.8.4 Reaction classification

Addition

An addition reaction adds atoms across a carbon-carbon double bond and forms one product：

\[ \ce{CH2=CH2 + H2 -> CH3CH3} \]

The C=C becomes a C-C single bond and a new bond forms to each carbon。

Substitution

A substitution reaction replaces one atom or group with another atom or group：

\[ \ce{CH3CHBrCH3 + NaCl -> CH3CHClCH3 + NaBr} \]

The bromine atom is replaced by chlorine。

Elimination

An elimination reaction removes a small molecule from a larger molecule，often forming a double bond：

\[ \ce{CH3CH2OH ->[heat] CH2=CH2 + H2O} \]

Oxidation and reduction

In organic chemistry：

  • oxidation is gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen；
  • reduction is loss of oxygen or gain of hydrogen。

For example，ethanol can be oxidised to ethanal：

\[ \ce{CH3CH2OH + [O] -> CH3CHO + H2O} \]

Ethanal can be reduced back to ethanol：

\[ \ce{CH3CHO + [H] -> CH3CH2OH} \]

1.8.5 Structural isomers

Definition

Structural isomers have the same molecular formula but different structural formulae。They may differ in carbon skeleton、functional-group position or functional group。

Chain isomerism

Chain isomers have different arrangements of the carbon skeleton。For C₄H₁₀：

\[ \ce{CH3CH2CH2CH3}\quad\text{butane} \]

and

\[ \ce{(CH3)3CH}\quad\text{2-methylpropane} \]

are chain isomers。

Position isomerism

Position isomers have the same carbon skeleton and functional group，but the functional group or multiple bond is in a different position。

Functional-group isomerism

Functional-group isomers have the same molecular formula but different functional groups。For example，compounds with formula C₂H₆O can be ethanol, an alcohol，or methoxymethane, an ether。

Always check that proposed isomers have the same molecular formula and that no structure is duplicated by rotation or by reversing the chain。

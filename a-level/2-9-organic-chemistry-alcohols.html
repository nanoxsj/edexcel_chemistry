2.9 Organic Chemistry Alcohols

Structure, reactions, oxidation and practical separation

2.9 Organic Chemistry Alcohols

本页对应 2.9 Organic Chemistry Alcohols.pdf.pdf。重点掌握 alcohol nomenclature、primary / secondary / tertiary classification、combustion、halogenation、dehydration、oxidation 和 aldehyde tests。专业词汇保留 English。

Learning objectives

  • name and draw alcohols using structural、displayed and skeletal formulae；
  • classify alcohols as primary、secondary or tertiary；
  • write equations for combustion、halogenation and dehydration；
  • predict products of primary、secondary and tertiary alcohol oxidation；
  • distinguish aldehydes and ketones using Fehling’s solution and Tollens’ reagent；
  • choose reflux or distillation depending on the desired oxidation product。

2.9.1 Alcohols - introduction

Functional group and nomenclature

Alcohols contain the hydroxyl functional group, -OH。Acyclic saturated monohydric alcohols have general formula：

\[ \ce{C_nH_{2n+1}OH} \]

Naming follows the alkane name with the ending -ol。The carbon chain must include the carbon bonded to -OH，and numbering gives the hydroxyl group the lowest possible locant。

Examples：

  • CH₃OH：methanol；
  • CH₃CH₂OH：ethanol；
  • CH₃CH₂CH₂OH：propan-1-ol；
  • CH₃CH(OH)CH₃：propan-2-ol。

Two hydroxyl groups give a diol；three give a triol。

Classification of alcohols

Classification depends on the number of carbon atoms attached to the carbon carrying the -OH group：

  • primary alcohol (1°)：OH carbon attached to one other carbon or alkyl group；
  • secondary alcohol (2°)：OH carbon attached to two other carbons；
  • tertiary alcohol (3°)：OH carbon attached to three other carbons。

Examples：propan-1-ol is primary；propan-2-ol is secondary；2-methylpropan-2-ol is tertiary。

2.9.2 Reactions of alcohols

Complete combustion

Alcohols burn in oxygen to form carbon dioxide and water：

\[ \ce{C2H5OH + 3O2 ->[heat] 2CO2 + 3H2O} \]

Complete combustion is strongly exothermic and alcohols can be used as fuels。

Conversion to halogenoalkanes

These reactions replace the hydroxyl group with a halogen atom。

Chlorination with phosphorus(V) chloride

Phosphorus(V) chloride reacts vigorously at room temperature，so no heating is required：

\[ \ce{CH3CH2CH2OH + PCl5 -> CH3CH2CH2Cl + POCl3 + HCl} \]

Steamy fumes of hydrogen chloride make this reaction a qualitative test for an -OH group。

Tertiary alcohols can also react with concentrated hydrochloric acid at room temperature：

\[ \ce{(CH3)3COH + HCl -> (CH3)3CCl + H2O} \]

This method is not generally suitable for primary and secondary alcohols under the same conditions。

Bromination

Warm the alcohol with potassium bromide and approximately 50% sulfuric acid。First：

\[ \ce{2KBr + H2SO4 -> K2SO4 + 2HBr} \]

Then hydrogen bromide reacts with the alcohol：

\[ \ce{CH3CH2CH2CH2OH + HBr -> CH3CH2CH2CH2Br + H2O} \]

More concentrated sulfuric acid is avoided because it can oxidise bromide ions to bromine and produce side reactions。

Iodination

Heat the alcohol under reflux with iodine and red phosphorus。Phosphorus(III) iodide is generated in situ：

\[ \ce{2P + 3I2 -> 2PI3} \]

For ethanol：

\[ \ce{3C2H5OH + 3PI3 -> 3C2H5I + H3PO3} \]

Dehydration to alkenes

Heat the alcohol with concentrated phosphoric acid。The -OH group and hydrogen atom on an adjacent carbon are removed，forming a C=C bond：

\[ \ce{CH3CH2OH ->[H3PO4][heat] CH2=CH2 + H2O} \]

This is an elimination / dehydration reaction。The acid acts as a catalyst and is not written as a reactant in the overall equation。

2.9.3 Oxidation of alcohols

Oxidising agent and colour change

Common oxidising agent：acidified potassium dichromate(VI)，K₂Cr₂O₇。The acid supplies H⁺ needed for the reduction half reaction。

During oxidation：

  • orange Cr₂O₇²⁻ is reduced to green Cr³⁺；
  • alcohol is oxidised according to its class；
  • mixture is usually warmed。

Primary alcohols

Primary alcohols oxidise first to aldehydes and then to carboxylic acids：

\[ \ce{RCH2OH + [O] -> RCHO + H2O} \]

\[ \ce{RCHO + [O] -> RCOOH} \]

For example：

\[ \ce{CH3CH2OH ->[O] CH3CHO ->[O] CH3COOH} \]

To obtain the aldehyde，use distillation with addition so the aldehyde is removed as soon as it forms。To obtain the carboxylic acid，use excess oxidising agent and reflux so aldehyde remains in the flask and is further oxidised。

Secondary alcohols

Secondary alcohols oxidise to ketones：

\[ \ce{R2CHOH + [O] -> R2C=O + H2O} \]

Example：

\[ \ce{CH3CH(OH)CH3 ->[O] CH3COCH3 + H2O} \]

Ketones do not oxidise further under these conditions，so the product does not need to be distilled off immediately。

Tertiary alcohols

Tertiary alcohols do not oxidise readily because the carbon carrying -OH has no hydrogen atom that can be removed to form a carbonyl group。Strong conditions may cause C-C bond cleavage rather than the standard alcohol oxidation seen for primary and secondary alcohols。

Alcohol class Product with acidified K₂Cr₂O₇
primary aldehyde, then carboxylic acid
secondary ketone
tertiary no reaction under standard conditions

2.9.4 Aldehyde tests

Fehling’s solution

Fehling’s solution is an alkaline solution containing Cu²⁺ ions。Warm with the unknown：

  • aldehyde is oxidised to carboxylate / carboxylic acid；
  • Cu²⁺ is reduced to Cu⁺；
  • clear blue solution gives an opaque red precipitate of Cu₂O；
  • ketones give a negative result。

Tollens’ reagent

Tollens’ reagent is alkaline silver nitrate in excess ammonia，also called ammoniacal silver nitrate。Warm with the unknown：

  • aldehyde is oxidised to carboxylate；
  • Ag⁺ is reduced to silver atoms；
  • a silver mirror forms on the inside of the test tube；
  • ketones give a negative result。

\[ \ce{Ag+ + e- -> Ag} \]

Both tests distinguish aldehydes from ketones because aldehydes are more easily oxidised。

2.9.5 Practical techniques

Heating under reflux

Use reflux when full oxidation is required：

  • primary alcohol → carboxylic acid；
  • secondary alcohol → ketone。

The vertical condenser cools vapour and returns it to the flask，preventing volatile reactants and products escaping while the mixture is heated。

Add anti-bumping granules before heating，and keep the top of the condenser open to the atmosphere。

Distillation with addition

Use distillation when partial oxidation is required，especially primary alcohol → aldehyde。Only the oxidising mixture is heated，and the alcohol is added slowly。The aldehyde has a lower boiling point than the starting alcohol and distils into the receiver as it forms。

Choosing the method

Desired product Method Reason
carboxylic acid from primary alcohol reflux with excess oxidising agent aldehyde remains and is oxidised further
aldehyde from primary alcohol distillation with addition aldehyde is removed before further oxidation
ketone from secondary alcohol reflux or distillation ketone is not readily oxidised further

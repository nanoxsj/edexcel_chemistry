2.8 Organic Chemistry Halogenoalkanes

Nomenclature, nucleophilic substitution, elimination and hydrolysis

2.8 Organic Chemistry Halogenoalkanes

本页对应 2.8 Organic Chemistry Halogenoalkanes.pdf.pdf。重点掌握 halogenoalkane nomenclature、primary / secondary / tertiary classification、nucleophilic substitution、elimination、hydrolysis、reactivity trends 和 C-X bond enthalpy。专业词汇保留 English。

Learning objectives

  • name and classify halogenoalkanes；
  • explain why the C-X bond is polar；
  • write equations for hydrolysis、nitrile formation、amine formation and elimination；
  • draw a nucleophilic substitution mechanism；
  • compare hydroxide、water、cyanide and ammonia as nucleophiles；
  • use silver halide precipitates to monitor hydrolysis；
  • explain reactivity trends using C-X bond enthalpy and primary / secondary / tertiary structure。

2.8.1 Halogenoalkanes - introduction

Naming

Halogenoalkanes are alkanes in which one or more hydrogen atoms are replaced by halogen atoms。Prefixes：

Halogen Prefix
fluorine fluoro-
chlorine chloro-
bromine bromo-
iodine iodo-

Naming steps：

  1. choose the longest carbon chain；
  2. number the chain to give substituents the lowest possible locants；
  3. use the correct halogen prefix；
  4. list different substituents alphabetically。

Examples：

  • CH₃CH₂CH₂Br：1-bromopropane；
  • CH₃CH(Br)CH₃：2-bromopropane；
  • CH₃CH(Cl)CH₂Br：1-bromo-2-chloropropane。

Classification

Classification depends on the number of carbon atoms attached to the carbon carrying the halogen：

  • primary (1°)：C-X carbon attached to one other carbon；
  • secondary (2°)：C-X carbon attached to two other carbons；
  • tertiary (3°)：C-X carbon attached to three other carbons。

你需要能够画 structural、displayed 和 skeletal formulae，并清楚标出 carbon attached to the halogen。

2.8.2 Reactions of halogenoalkanes

Nucleophiles

A nucleophile is an electron-rich species that donates a pair of electrons to an electron-deficient centre。Because the C-X bond is polar，carbon carries a partial positive charge and is attacked by nucleophiles。

常见 nucleophiles：OH⁻、CN⁻、NH₃ and H₂O。

Formation of alcohols - hydrolysis with hydroxide

Warm aqueous sodium hydroxide / potassium hydroxide is used：

\[ \ce{CH3CH2Br + OH- -> CH3CH2OH + Br-} \]

This is nucleophilic substitution and also a hydrolysis reaction。The halogen is replaced by OH。

Hydroxide is a better nucleophile than water because it has a full formal negative charge；oxygen in water carries only partial negative charge。

Water can also act as nucleophile，but reaction is much slower：

\[ \ce{RX + H2O -> ROH + H+ + X-} \]

Acidified ethanolic silver nitrate can be used to detect the halide ions as they form：

\[ \ce{Ag+ + X- -> AgX(s)} \]

Formation of nitriles

Heat a halogenoalkane under reflux with ethanolic potassium cyanide：

\[ \ce{CH3CH2Br + CN- -> CH3CH2CN + Br-} \]

The product is propanenitrile。The CN group adds one carbon atom，so this is a useful carbon-chain extension reaction。

Conditions：ethanolic KCN、heat under reflux。The cyanide ion is the nucleophile。

Formation of primary amines

Heat a primary halogenoalkane under pressure with excess ethanolic ammonia：

\[ \ce{CH3CH2Br + 2NH3 -> CH3CH2NH2 + NH4Br} \]

The product is ethylamine。Excess ammonia is used to reduce further substitution because the amine product is itself a nucleophile and can react again to form secondary / tertiary amines or quaternary ammonium salts。

Formation of alkenes - elimination

Heat a halogenoalkane with ethanolic sodium hydroxide：

\[ \ce{CH3CH2Br + OH- ->[ethanol][heat] CH2=CH2 + H2O + Br-} \]

This is elimination：a hydrogen atom and halogen are removed from adjacent carbon atoms，and a C=C bond forms。Ethanolic hydroxide favours elimination；aqueous hydroxide favours nucleophilic substitution。

2.8.3 Nucleophilic substitution mechanism

Polar C-X bond

Halogens are more electronegative than carbon，so the bonding pair in C-X is pulled toward X：

\[ \ce{C^{delta+}-X^{delta-}} \]

The carbon is electrophilic and the halogen can leave as X⁻ when the bond undergoes heterolytic fission。

Mechanism with hydroxide

For bromoethane and aqueous hydroxide：

  1. lone pair on OH⁻ attacks the δ+ carbon；
  2. a new C-O bond forms；
  3. C-Br bond breaks heterolytically；
  4. bromide ion leaves。

The curly arrow starts at the hydroxide lone pair and ends at the carbon。The second curly arrow starts at the C-Br bond and ends on bromine。

\[ \ce{CH3CH2Br + OH- -> CH3CH2OH + Br-} \]

Mechanism with ammonia

Ammonia has a lone pair and acts as a neutral nucleophile。The mechanism can be represented in two stages：

  1. NH₃ attacks the partial positive carbon and forms an alkylammonium ion；
  2. a second NH₃ molecule acts as a base and removes a proton。

Overall：

\[ \ce{RBr + NH3 -> [RNH3]+Br-} \]

\[ \ce{[RNH3]+Br- + NH3 -> RNH2 + NH4Br} \]

2.8.4 Hydrolysis of halogenoalkanes

Measuring the rate

Acidified aqueous silver nitrate can compare rates of hydrolysis：

  1. place ethanol and acidified silver nitrate in three test tubes in a 50 °C water bath；
  2. add equal drops of chloroalkane、bromoalkane and iodoalkane；
  3. start a stopwatch；
  4. record time for silver halide precipitate to appear。

The halide ions form as hydrolysis occurs and react with silver ions：

\[ \ce{Ag+(aq) + X-(aq) -> AgX(s)} \]

Halogenoalkane Precipitate Relative hydrolysis rate
chloroalkane white AgCl slowest
bromoalkane cream AgBr intermediate
iodoalkane pale yellow AgI fastest

The faster the precipitate forms，the faster the C-X bond is broken and the greater the halogenoalkane reactivity。

Fair test variables

To compare halogenoalkanes fairly，keep constant：

  • volume and concentration of silver nitrate；
  • volume of ethanol；
  • temperature；
  • number of drops / amount of halogenoalkane；
  • acid concentration；
  • test-tube size and observation method。

Use a water bath rather than direct flame because ethanol is flammable。Repeat and compare mean times。

2.8.5 Trends in halogenoalkanes

Halogen trend

Experimental reactivity trend：

\[ \text{fluoroalkanes} < \text{chloroalkanes} < \text{bromoalkanes} < \text{iodoalkanes} \]

Iodoalkanes react fastest；fluoroalkanes are least reactive and generally do not hydrolyse under these conditions。

Bond enthalpy explanation

Although C-F is the most polar bond，bond strength is the dominant factor for substitution rate：

Bond Bond enthalpy / kJ mol⁻¹ Relative strength
C-F 467 strongest
C-Cl 346
C-Br 290
C-I 228 weakest

The C-I bond requires least energy to break，so iodide leaves most readily。The C-F bond is strongest，so fluoroalkanes are least reactive。

The carbon-halogen bond becomes less polar down the group because electronegativity difference decreases，but this effect is outweighed by the much lower bond enthalpy of C-I。

Primary, secondary and tertiary halogenoalkanes

Using the same halogen and same molecular formula，hydrolysis rate also depends on structure：

\[ \text{tertiary} > \text{secondary} > \text{primary} \]

In the conditions used for hydrolysis，tertiary halogenoalkanes react fastest and primary halogenoalkanes slowest。The difference is associated with the available nucleophilic-substitution pathways, commonly described using S_N1 and S_N2 mechanisms at A Level。
